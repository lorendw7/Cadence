# QREC pitch and questions

Prepared: 2026-11-12. This is a rehearsal guide for the submitted Cadence application, not an award notice or a product release. The questions below are practice questions, not an official question list. English participation still needs confirmation from QREC.

## One-minute introduction

Hello, I'm Shandong. I'm building Cadence, a free planner for study and daily life.

I find it hard to connect my class schedule with my daily plans. Some classmates want an easier timetable experience too.

Cadence aims to show classes, tasks and free time clearly. Students have different habits, and I want to give them another useful choice.

I have built an early task prototype. Over eight weeks, I will talk with students, make a timetable prototype and improve it through their feedback.

With QREC's advice and support, I hope to make daily planning easier for students. Thank you.

## Practice questions

### 1. What problem are you trying to solve?

Checking a class schedule and planning the rest of the day can feel like separate jobs. I want to help students see their classes, tasks and free time together. For example, they could find a free afternoon and plan time for an assignment.

### 2. How is Cadence different from a calendar or timetable app?

I want to focus on the step from checking classes to planning the day. A clear today view and week view may help with that. I will compare existing tools and learn where Cadence can add useful help. I am not claiming that every student needs a new app.

### 3. Why do you want to build this?

In my own use, the university system feels dated. I would like a clearer and more comfortable way to check classes and plan my time. Some classmates have shared similar wishes. That gave me a reason to start building Cadence.

### 4. What have you built so far?

I have an early Flutter prototype. It can add tasks, mark them as done, delete them and undo the latest deletion. The data is only kept while the app is running. Saving, the timetable view and automatic updates are not built yet.

### 5. What will you do in eight weeks?

First, I will learn about students' routines through a short survey and conversations. Then I will make a small timetable prototype with sample data. Students will try it, and I will improve the confusing parts. I will finish with a demo, findings and a clear next-step plan.

### 6. How will you know whether it helps?

I will watch students use it for everyday activities, such as finding a class or planning study time. I will ask what helps, what is missing and where they get stuck. I will group the feedback by need, improve the design and try it again. Different preferences can help me understand who it is useful for.

### 7. Do you already have evidence of demand?

Several classmates have told me they want an easier timetable experience. Some would like automatic import or updates. These are informal comments. I still need to learn more from students with different routines, including people outside my friends.

### 8. Will it connect to the university system?

There is no university connection in the current plan. The early tests will use sample schedules. I can study students' interest in easier import or updates without connecting to school systems. A school connection would need a separate decision and permission.

### 9. Why do you need a server and Google Play registration?

These are optional cost estimates in my application. The core planner is intended to work locally. A server would only be needed for a specific remote test, and Google Play registration would only be needed for a suitable Android test. I will confirm both with QREC and remove them if they are not needed during the funded period.

### 10. How much support are you requesting?

My draft budget is 20,000 yen: 6,000 for a test server, 5,000 for Google Play registration, 8,000 for participant thank-you payments and 1,000 for materials. These are estimates. I will agree on eligible costs and payment methods with QREC before spending.

### 11. Can you manage this alone?

I will keep the work small and focus on one useful planning experience. I can lead the design and development, and use student feedback to decide what to improve. I would also welcome collaborators and advice from QREC. The eight-week goal is a tested prototype, not a full public release.

### 12. Will it be free, and which devices will it support?

The plan is to offer the same free features to everyone, with no paid tier. I am working toward a desktop version first. Web and mobile versions are later directions. An Android launch is not the goal of these eight weeks.

### 13. What support do you want besides money?

I would like advice on asking useful questions, understanding feedback and keeping the project realistic. I also hope to meet students who are interested in design or development. Mentoring would help me make better decisions as I build.

## Useful phrases during questions

- Could you please repeat the question more slowly?
- Could you explain what you mean by that?
- Do you mean the current prototype or the future plan?
- I have not confirmed that yet. I will check with QREC.
- That part is not built yet. It is a later idea.
- That is a useful point. I will consider it in the next design.

## Rehearsal and accuracy notes

- Read the introduction aloud with a timer. Aim for 50-55 seconds to leave room for pauses. This is a practice target, not a measured speaking time. If it runs long, shorten the text instead of speaking faster.
- Pause after each paragraph. Remember five points: problem, idea, current prototype, eight-week plan and support needed.
- In questions, give the direct answer first, then one example. Short answers are easier to follow; expand if asked.
- Keep feedback useful without imposing a fixed recruitment target or a majority-preference threshold.
- Recheck the current prototype before the event. Do not describe saving, timetable features, import or sync as working until they are implemented and checked.
- The Google Play estimate is a budget allowance, not a confirmed yen price. Server and store costs remain conditional; they do not move Android or cloud work into the desktop delivery milestone.
- Confirm English pitch, question and mentoring arrangements with QREC. Also confirm insurance details and expense rules. No approval for these items is recorded here.
- Keep application attachments, student numbers, contact details and private correspondence outside this public repository.
